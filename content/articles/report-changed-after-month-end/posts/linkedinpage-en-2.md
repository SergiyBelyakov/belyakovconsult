Last month's report shows a new number. The first question in the meeting is usually "who broke the report?" Most of the time, nobody.

Three reasons the past changes in data (illustrative examples):

• A late record. An application submitted on 28 October was entered into the system on 12 November. It belongs to October, so October "grew".
• A status change. On 5 November the application was approved, on 20 November it was rejected. It dropped out of the report on approved applications.
• Merged duplicates. After cleaning, three records of one person became one, and past numbers went down.

Add retroactive date corrections and reference data changes, when a unit moves to another region together with its whole history.

All of this is normal, correct data work. So looking for someone to blame is pointless. A better question: why does the report not know about these changes, and why can nobody say which of the two numbers is official?

The answer is no longer in analytics but in how the data is stored. More on that tomorrow.
