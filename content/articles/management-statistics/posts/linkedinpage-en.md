Many reports. No decisions.

Picture a monthly management report with forty indicators. Everyone opens it, nobody acts on it. The problem is rarely the data. It is that no number in it is tied to a decision.

Management statistics is not a bigger report. It is a short set of indicators, and each one answers three questions:
• Decision: which decision does this number serve?
• Owner: who acts when it moves?
• Threshold: at what value do we act?

An illustrative example. "Applications processed per month" goes up and down, and the report shows it faithfully. Add a decision (do we move staff between offices next month?), an owner (the head of operations) and a threshold (a backlog of more than two weeks in any office). The same number becomes a management tool rather than decoration.

It works like a fuel gauge: it matters because you know what to do when it drops. (The analogy has limits: in a car nobody argues about what "fuel" means. In an organisation that definition has to be agreed first.)

A 20-minute check: take five indicators from your latest management report and fill in decision, owner and threshold for each. Whatever stays blank is a candidate to redesign or remove.
