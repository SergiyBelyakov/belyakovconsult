The register shows three service recipients. In fact it is one person.

Entered once correctly. Once with an extra dot after the first name. Once with a different Latin spelling, because that is how another office does it. Illustrative, but familiar to anyone who has worked with a large register.

Duplicates rarely come from carelessness. More often they are a product of the process:
• typos at entry
• transliteration with several "correct" versions
• a new document, and nobody found the old record
• several entry points, none of which checks whether the person already exists

The effects are not cosmetic. The coverage report is inflated. One person can receive two payments. Or the opposite: the payment is tied to the old record while the person comes through the new one.

It is like a phone book with one person saved three times. Inconvenient, but you can live with it. (The analogy has limits: your phone does not send money to each contact.)

The most expensive way to fight this is cleaning duplicates in the report every month. The cheapest is teaching the entry form, once, to show a similar existing record.
