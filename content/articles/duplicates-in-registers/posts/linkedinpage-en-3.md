Finding a duplicate in a register is easy. Deciding what to do with it is harder.

A "merge" button does not answer the questions that money and trust in the register depend on:
• Which record remains the master record, and on what principle: the oldest, the most complete, the one confirmed by a document?
• What happens to the history of payments and services on the other records?
• Who approves a merge when a payment depends on it?
• How do other systems that hold references to the old record learn about the change?

This is a data governance question, not only a technical one. Without a data owner, duplicate cleaning quickly turns into an argument about whose record is more correct.

One rule I would add first: duplicates are not deleted. A deleted record takes the history of applications, decisions and payments with it. It is marked as merged, with a reference to the master.

And to stop new duplicates, the entry form should show a similar record before saving: "A similar record already exists. Is this the same person?"

A question for you: do you know how many people in your register have more than one record?
