41,200 or 38,900? Two teams bring different numbers of recipients for the same quarter, and both are right.

The example is illustrative, but the situation is familiar. The gap is almost never about arithmetic. Usually teams count slightly different things under the same name:

• one counts people, the other applications
• one uses the end-of-quarter status, the other the report date
• one takes data from the register, the other from its own spreadsheet

It is like a football match with two referees, each holding a different rulebook. (The analogy has limits: an organisation has to write its own rules, often mid-season.) Agreeing on those rules is what data governance is.

It does not start with a hundred-page policy. It starts with four questions for every critical indicator:
1. Who sets the rule?
2. Which source is authoritative?
3. Who fixes an error?
4. Who approves a change?

Try it on your last number conflict. Wherever the answer sounds like "it depends who you ask", that is where to start.
