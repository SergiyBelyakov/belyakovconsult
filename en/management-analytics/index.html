When it is needed

The indicators exist. A shared management view often does not.

Each team sees only its own part of the picture

The indicators exist, but there is no common logic that allows comparison across teams, regions or programmes.

Too many reports, too little focus

Decisions arrive late

Data is collected manually, monitoring lags behind operations and the signal appears too late.

What I do

From scattered tables to a management system of indicators

The goal is not to add one more dashboard. Management analytics should make internal enterprise processes visible, comparable and manageable.

  • Control of plans, tasks and internal commitments.
  • Analysis of procurement, approvals, deadlines and key operating workflows.
  • Assessment of team, unit and resource workload.
  • KPI design for internal processes and control of deviations from plan.
  • Comparison across units, periods and business areas using one set of rules.
  • Management dashboards and concise executive reviews.
Output

An output that management can actually use

  • an aligned KPI set and calculation rules
  • a management dashboard or regular executive report
  • comparisons across units, regions or periods
  • visibility of bottlenecks and deviations
  • a short list of decisions supported by data
How I work

Management logic first. Visualisation comes second.

01

Question

We define which decisions the analytics must support and which signals are critical.

02

Indicators

We agree KPI logic, breakdown dimensions, refresh frequency and roles.

03

Model

We assemble the sources into one comparable indicator system.

04

Validation

We make sure the indicators are understood in the same way by all parties and match the sources.

05

Decision

The final view is delivered as a dashboard, report or short executive review.
