01

The numbers do not add up

The dashboard looks reassuring, but your team sees a different picture. The first step is to find where the discrepancy starts.

02

The rules have changed

The same indicator is calculated differently over time. Before comparing results, you need to check the definitions.

03

Everyone has their own spreadsheet

Departments keep separate files. Meetings turn into discussions about whose numbers are correct.

04

Errors build up

Duplicates, missing values and faulty transformations reach your reports. The totals hide the underlying problems.

05

The source is unclear

A number appears in the report, but nobody can show the records and calculations behind it.

06

Manual corrections

Someone adjusts the data before every report. Knowledge of those changes stays with one person.

07

Results need evidence

Partners and funders need sources, calculations and supporting records. A dashboard alone is not enough.

08

Data is not helping decisions

When people do not trust the numbers, they rely on intuition. Analytics becomes another report to file away.

From checking the numbers to a working system

Diagnosis

Find the cause of discrepancies

I review indicator definitions, queries and sources. You receive a list of issues, their impact on reporting and a clear order for fixing them.

Architecture

Put data and processes in order

I design data models, relationships and quality checks. The goal is a clear path from source to report, with less manual work.

Monitoring and evaluation

Build reporting backed by evidence

I help align indicators, data collection and supporting records so your team can explain every result it reports.

Examples of work

Social policy

I develop information and analytics systems, establish data quality management processes and configure fraud analysis to identify suspicious patterns and records that need further review.

Focus: reliable data, quality controls and fraud risk analysis.

Access to healthcare

I develop analytical dashboards and support the implementation of the Electronic Healthcare System (eHealth) in primary and emergency care. I analyse the national healthcare facility network using geospatial methods and build a composite facility assessment based on 44 indicators.

Scope: dashboards, eHealth, geospatial analysis and assessment across 44 indicators.

Programme monitoring

Programme data is scattered across files and teams. The task is to connect indicators, reports and supporting documents.

Deliverable: a traceable path from source to reported result.
