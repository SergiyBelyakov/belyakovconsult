Data Analysis

Find the answer in the data, not another pile of numbers

Data Analysis starts with a question, not a chart. We define the decision first, then prepare and test the data, look for patterns and check whether the conclusion survives scrutiny.

Discuss a taskWhat I analyse
When a separate analysis is useful

The data exists. The answer does not yet.

Many tables, no common picture

Data sits in spreadsheets, registers or systems, but the measures do not yet add up to one defensible answer.

The average hides the problem

The overall figure looks acceptable while specific groups, places or periods behave very differently.

A chart shows what happened, not why

The task is to examine factors, segments, relationships and anomalies rather than repeat the same number visually.

Scope

From raw data to a checked conclusion

I do not use a complex method simply because it is available. The method has to fit the question, data quality and the cost of being wrong.

  • Data preparation, normalisation and profiling.
  • Anomaly, missing value, duplicate and distribution analysis.
  • Comparison across groups, locations, periods, plan and actual.
  • Segmentation, trend analysis and examination of factors that change the outcome.
  • Statistical, financial, management and geospatial analysis where the question requires it.
  • Visualisation of findings with explicit data limitations.
Output

A result that can be checked

  • prepared analytical dataset
  • documented calculation and analytical logic
  • findings with tested assumptions
  • charts, tables or a dashboard
  • short decision note supported by the evidence
