What holds back informed decisions

01

Conflicting figures

Institutions, regional teams and partners report different figures. Leaders need shared definitions and a consistent basis for decisions.

02

Unclear indicators

Activities are counted, but changes for people remain unclear. Indicators need to reflect what the programme is meant to achieve.

03

Fragmented registers

Records sit in separate systems and spreadsheets. Teams struggle to build a complete picture of services and the people they reach.

04

Unreliable data

Duplicates and missing records distort coverage and needs assessments. Quality checks need to catch problems before reporting.

05

Unclear ownership

Nobody knows who approves an indicator or resolves a data issue. Clear rules, accountable owners and working procedures are needed.

06

Manual reporting

Teams spend each month consolidating files. Little time remains to explain deviations or prepare recommendations for management.

07

Results without evidence

Partners and funders need to verify reported results. Every indicator needs a documented source and calculation method.

08

Competing priorities

Where should services expand and resources go? Decisions need comparable evidence on needs, access and results across locations.

Data Architecture

Data Architecture

Architecture is not a diagram for its own sake. It should explain how data moves from a source system into an indicator, a report, an exchange or an API.

An architecture review, a target design or support through the transition. Technology choices follow the operating problem, not fashion.

Open service
Data Quality

Data Quality

Data quality is not one abstract percentage. The useful question is which errors can change a payment, coverage estimate, report or management conclusion.

A one off diagnostic or a recurring control process. Checks are tied to business rules and operational risk, not to a cosmetic quality score.

Open service
Data Governance

Data Governance

Good governance answers very ordinary questions. Who sets the rule. Who fixes an error. Which source is authoritative. Who approves a change.

An operating model, roles and rules without turning governance into paperwork. Start with critical data and real conflicts, not a hundred page policy.

Open service
Geospatial Analysis

Geospatial Analysis

Geospatial analysis shows where a service exists on paper but is difficult to reach in practice. Distance, travel time, demand, capacity and service areas tell a different story from a regional average.

A focused study, maps or a geospatial dashboard. The result should help decide where a network or resource priority needs to change.
