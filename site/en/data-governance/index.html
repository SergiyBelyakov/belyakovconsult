Data Governance

Shared data still needs a named person who can make a decision

Good governance answers very ordinary questions. Who sets the rule. Who fixes an error. Which source is authoritative. Who approves a change.

When this becomes a problem

Data Governance

One indicator has several “correct” values

Teams use different formulas, periods or sources and end up with incompatible numbers.

Everyone knows the issue, nobody owns it

There is no data owner or data steward with the authority and responsibility to take the problem through to resolution.

Rules live in people’s heads

Definitions, exceptions and calculation logic disappear when the team changes.

What we work on

What the work covers

You do not judge governance by the number of policies. You see it when there is a conflict. The team knows who decides, which rule applies and where that logic is recorded.

  • Define data domains, data owners, data stewards and clear areas of responsibility.
  • Build a business glossary and a shared dictionary of indicators, fields and terms.
  • Set rules for creating, changing, validating and using critical data.
  • Create a practical process for data issues from registration through remediation and closure.
  • Connect governance with data quality, metadata, lineage, access controls and BI.
What you get

What you get

  • a data governance operating model
  • a roles and responsibilities matrix
  • a business glossary and critical data element list
  • an issue and change process
  • a phased launch plan without unnecessary bureaucracy
